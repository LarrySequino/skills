The volume of pages received in a single week reached 900. The author reviewed the full set on a Sunday, having been unable to sleep, and by approximately page 400 had ceased reading the accompanying text. This is arguably the most significant detail in the account.

An analysis of the count showed that 812 of those pages originated from the same three alerts. The first was a disk utilization alert on the log host, triggered above 80 percent, a threshold that has been exceeded since 2023 without incident and may have been safe to disregard. The second was a p99 latency alert configured to fire on any individual request slower than two seconds, a condition the batch export produces at 04:00 daily as a matter of design. The third was a synthetic check that continued to probe a health endpoint deprecated in March.

The underlying issue was therefore not the alerting system itself, but rather the absence of any process for retiring obsolete alerts.

This distinction is relevant because it determines the appropriate remedy. Threshold tuning can be pursued for an extended period without preventing pages at 4am, since the thresholds were not the operative factor. Rules must instead be deleted. It should be noted that deletion tends to be less popular than tuning.

A total of 41 rules were deleted in a single afternoon, and 12 were retained. The criterion applied was straightforward: any rule that had fired more than twice without ever prompting action was classified as a notification rather than an alert, and notifications are appropriately routed to a channel rather than to a phone. This approach is not novel. The SRE book has advocated it since 2016. In some cases, reading a recommendation and implementing it on a given Thursday are somewhat different undertakings.

Page volume in the following week totaled 31. Of the original 900 pages, 13 had been actionable, representing 1.4 percent; of the 31, 6 were actionable, representing approximately 19 percent. Sleep quality improved for the author and for Ruth, who held the subsequent rotation. The more substantial change was that team members resumed reading pages. When the pager carries meaning, it receives attention, and when it does not, personnel learn to disregard it.

Two adjustments would be made in retrospect. The acted-on counts should have been compiled prior to the meeting rather than argued from memory, as the counts resolved the discussion in four minutes whereas the memory-based version had persisted since February. Additionally, the deletion review should have been scheduled to recur. The rule count has already returned to 19, and three of those rules appear familiar.
