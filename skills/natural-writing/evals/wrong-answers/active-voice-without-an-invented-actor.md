# Lot 9 arsenic, and why the plate got run twice

Short version: we sent numbers 18 percent low on Wednesday, we caught it Thursday, and we
sent the corrected set out Friday at 11. Nobody downstream had acted on the bad numbers. I
am writing the rest of this for the file, because if we leave it at "the reagent lot
changed" it will happen again in the spring.

## Methods

We collected twenty-four samples along the Kettle Creek transect at three depths: surface,
one meter, and 20 cm above the sediment. We held the samples at 4 degrees C and digested
them within six hours of collection. We digested aliquots in 5 mL of nitric acid at 95
degrees C for 45 minutes, then diluted them to 50 mL. We determined total arsenic by hydride
generation AAS at 193.7 nm on a 10 second integration. Every plate carried two blanks and a
20 ppb spike.

## What went wrong

Reagent lot 4471 arrived on the 14th and went straight onto the bench. We did not re-run the
calibration curve against it. Wren started the plate anyway on the Tuesday morning with the
March curve still loaded, and the spike came back at 82 percent. That figure sat on the run
sheet the whole time. We read it as a bad spike rather than a bad curve, and Halim signed
off the run before lunch.

I found the buffer cloudy when I got to it on Thursday, which is what made me look at the
curve at all. Cloudy buffer is a five minute problem. The curve was a two day problem.

A night technician left the chest freezer in 2B open overnight that same week. Three groups
share that room, and nobody had signed the door log since Monday. The freezer held the
reagents under 0 degrees C when I checked, so nothing in these results depends on it, and I
am putting it in here because the same door stood open in February.

## What we changed

A lot change now blocks the queue until a fresh curve runs against it. Wren wrote that check
into the plate template on Friday. It took her about an hour and it would have saved us four
days. Halim and I both initial the run sheet now, and one of us has to write the spike
recovery on it in ink before either of us signs.

The run had already finished by the time any of this became obvious. That is the part worth
sitting with. We had every control we needed: present, printed, and ignored.
