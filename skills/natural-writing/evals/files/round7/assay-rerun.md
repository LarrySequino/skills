# Lot 9 arsenic, and why the plate got run twice

Short version: the numbers we sent Wednesday were 18 percent low, we caught it
Thursday, and the corrected set went out Friday at 11. Nobody downstream had acted
on the bad numbers. The rest of this is for the file, because if we leave it at
"the reagent lot changed" it will happen again in the spring.

## Methods

Twenty-four samples were collected along the Kettle Creek transect at three depths:
surface, one meter, and 20 cm above the sediment. Samples were held at 4 degrees C
and digested within six hours of collection. Aliquots were digested in 5 mL of
nitric acid at 95 degrees C for 45 minutes, then diluted to 50 mL. Total arsenic
was determined by hydride generation AAS at 193.7 nm on a 10 second integration.
Every plate carried two blanks and a 20 ppb spike.

## What went wrong

Reagent lot 4471 arrived on the 14th and went straight onto the bench. The
calibration curve was not re-run against it. The plate was started anyway on the
Tuesday morning with the March curve still loaded, and the spike came back at
82 percent. That figure sat on the run sheet the whole time. It was read as a bad
spike rather than a bad curve, and the run was signed off before lunch.

The buffer was cloudy by the time I got to it on Thursday, which is what made me
look at the curve at all. Cloudy buffer is a five minute problem. The curve was a
two day problem.

The chest freezer in 2B was left open overnight that same week. Three groups share
that room, the door log had not been signed since Monday, and I have no idea whose
door it was. The reagents in it were still under 0 degrees C when I checked, so
nothing in these results depends on it, and I am putting it in here because the
same door was open in February.

## What we changed

A lot change now blocks the queue until a fresh curve runs against it. Wren wrote
that check into the plate template on Friday. It took her about an hour and it
would have saved us four days. Halim and I both initial the run sheet now, and the
spike recovery has to be written on it in ink before either of us does.

The run was already over by the time any of this was obvious. That is the part
worth sitting with. Every control we needed was present, printed, and ignored.
