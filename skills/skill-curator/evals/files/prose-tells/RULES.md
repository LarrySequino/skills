# Rules

Ordered by how often I end up writing the note.

## 1. The apologetic hedge before a real claim

A sentence that spends its first clause lowering expectations and its second clause making
a confident assertion. "While this is admittedly a small sample, the effect is unmistakable."
Cut the first clause or cut the certainty in the second. Keeping both is a machine trying to
be right either way.

## 2. Paragraph-final restatement

The last sentence of a paragraph that repeats the first sentence in different words. Human
writers move on; generators close the loop. Delete the last sentence and see whether anything
was lost.

## 3. The invisible narrator

"It should be noted that", "it is worth mentioning", "one might argue". Somebody is doing the
noting, the mentioning, and the arguing, and it is the writer. Say the thing.

## 4. Symmetric list items

Three bullets of the same length with the same grammatical shape, especially when the third
one is padded to match. Real lists are lumpy. If every item is a nine-word noun phrase, at
least one of them was invented to fill the pattern.

## 5. Escalating adjective pairs

"Robust and scalable", "clear and actionable", "thoughtful and deliberate". The second word
almost never adds anything the first did not carry. Pick one.

## 6. The stakes paragraph

A closing paragraph explaining why the subject matters, aimed at a reader who is already
three pages in and therefore already knows. Delete it and the piece usually gets better.

## 7. Numbers with no provenance

"Roughly 40% of teams" with no source, no date, no method. A specific number is a promise
that someone counted. If nobody counted, use "most" and be honest about it.

## 8. Reference-book voice in a personal note

Second person disappearing into an impersonal register halfway through — "you should
consider" becoming "one should consider" becoming "it is advisable to". Pick a distance
and hold it.

## 9. The em dash as a comma substitute

Not a rule against em dashes. A rule against using one where a comma would do, which is
how you end up with four in a paragraph and a page that reads as generated.

## 10. Concluding transitions on a piece with no argument

"Ultimately", "at the end of the day", "when all is said and done" at the head of a
paragraph that concludes nothing. The transition is doing the work the reasoning did not.
